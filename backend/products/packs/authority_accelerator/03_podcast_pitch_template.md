# Podcast Pitch Template

A cold-pitch template for landing podcast guest spots in your niche.
Hit rate at typical operator stage: 5-15% acceptance — meaning a
10-pitch-per-week cadence yields 5-8 bookings per quarter.

## The principle: pitch the EPISODE, not yourself

Most podcast pitches sound like resumes. "Hi, I'm Jane. I do X. I'd
love to come on your show." These get deleted.

The pitches that get accepted pitch a *specific episode topic* with
a *specific reason this audience needs it now*. The host's job is
producing episodes, not auditioning experts. Make their job easier.

## The pitch email

Subject: `[specific episode topic] for [podcast name]`

```
Hey [host first name],

Long-time listener of [podcast name] — episode [N] on [topic] was
particularly sharp. The point about [specific thing from that
episode] is one I've been thinking about a lot in my own work.

I have an episode pitch I think your audience would dig:

EPISODE: [Specific working title — outcome-focused, not abstract.
e.g., "Why most outbound sales sequences are mathematically
guaranteed to fail (and what to do instead)"]

WHY NOW: [One sentence on why this is timely or counter-current to
recent industry conversation. e.g., "Most of the recent advice
on sequence design assumes warm leads — but cold-outbound math has
fundamentally different conversion curves, and the advice
backfires when applied directly."]

WHAT YOUR AUDIENCE WILL GET: [3 bullet points — specific takeaways,
not vague claims. e.g.,
- The 3 conversion benchmarks that separate cold from warm sequences
- A 7-touch cadence specifically tuned for cold-outbound (with
  the diminishing-return math)
- The "second-no" reactivation play that recovers 12% of closed-lost
  cold leads]

ABOUT ME: I'm [Your name], [1-line credibility marker — e.g., "the
person behind the [Framework Name] approach, written about in
[publication] and tested across [N] B2B engagements."] [One more line
of specificity — a key result, a notable client, a recognizable
former employer.]

If the topic angle isn't right but you'd consider a different one,
happy to pitch 2-3 alternatives. And if now isn't the moment, I'll
circle back in [N] months — no pressure.

— [Your name]
[Your one-pager link]
[Your podcast clip reel link if you have one]
```

## How to research a podcast before pitching

15 minutes per podcast. Don't skip this step — generic pitches get
spotted instantly.

1. **Listen to 1 recent episode (skim at 1.5x).** Identify: the
   host's voice, the typical episode length, the kind of guests
   they book (operators? academics? executives?), the kind of
   topics they prefer (tactical? strategic? story-driven?).
2. **Check the last 6 episode titles.** What's been covered?
   Don't pitch a topic that's identical to a recent one.
3. **Identify the host's preferred contact path.** Their newsletter
   sometimes has a "want to be a guest?" link. Twitter / LinkedIn
   DMs are sometimes more responsive than email. Use the channel
   THEY prefer.
4. **Note one specific detail** — a recent episode that resonated,
   a guest you admired, a position the host has taken. Use it in
   the opening line of the pitch. NEVER use "I love your show" —
   it's lazy and they've heard it 1000 times.

## Follow-up cadence (when they don't respond)

A no-response is not a no. Follow up.

- **+10 days:** soft bump. "Wanted to make sure my pitch from [date]
  didn't get lost in the shuffle. Happy to send 2-3 alternative
  episode angles if the original isn't quite right."
- **+25 days:** value-add. Share a specific resource (article, data
  point, tool) related to their show's focus. No pitch.
- **+60 days:** repitch. New episode angle, new subject line.
  Reference the prior pitch briefly.

After three no-responses across 60+ days, retire the pitch and
move on. Re-approach in 6 months if your positioning has evolved
meaningfully.

## What to do after acceptance

Once a host says yes, you have 1-3 weeks before recording. Use them:

1. **Send the pre-recording prep email** — agenda, the 3-5 questions
   you'd love to be asked (most hosts welcome this), any data you'd
   like to reference.
2. **Re-listen to 2 of their episodes** — internalize the host's
   pacing + favorite types of question. You'll perform better.
3. **Prepare 2-3 "anchor quotables"** — sentences you've thought
   about in advance that capture your main idea in 1-2 lines. These
   become the show's clip-able moments.
4. **Set up the recording environment**: wired headphones, quiet
   room, water nearby, phone on do-not-disturb. Sound quality is
   half the perceived authority.

## What to do after airing

1. **Share within 24 hours** — your social channels, your newsletter
   if you have one, your email signature for 30 days.
2. **Thank the host publicly** — tag them, link them, share a
   specific moment from the episode you enjoyed.
3. **Send a private thank-you** + ask if they'd be willing to refer
   you to 1-2 other podcasts that book guests in your space.

## What kills podcast pitches

1. **"I'd love to come on your show."** Pitch the episode, not your
   ego.
2. **Generic credibility lines.** "I've been featured in many
   publications" → name the publications.
3. **Length.** A pitch over 200 words won't get fully read.
4. **No specific topic.** "I could talk about marketing." Pitch a
   *titled* episode.
5. **Pitching wildly off-format.** A 60-minute deep-dive show is
   not going to book your "10 quick tips" angle. Match their
   format.
