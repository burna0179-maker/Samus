# Speaker Bureau One-Pager

The forwardable PDF that converts "we should have her speak" into
"she's booked." Built once, used hundreds of times.

## What it is, structurally

A single page (yes, ONE page) PDF designed to be:

1. Forwarded internally inside event-organizer companies without
   needing your explanation.
2. Skimmable in 30 seconds.
3. Decisive — by the end, the reader knows whether to book you and
   how to do it.

## The exact layout

The page is divided into 6 zones. Top-to-bottom:

### Zone 1 — The hero strip (top 15% of page)

- **Your name** (large, bold, sans-serif)
- **Your one-line positioning** under your name — e.g., "Cadence
  engineer for B2B outbound" or "The [Framework Name] inventor"
- **A professional headshot** — color, eye contact, neutral
  background. If you don't have a good one, hire a photographer.
  This is the only zone where a professional designer + photographer
  pays for itself 10x.

### Zone 2 — The signature talks (next 35% of page)

Three to five named talks. Each:
- **Talk title** (8-12 words, outcome-focused)
- **3-bullet summary** — what the audience leaves with
- **Format** — keynote / panel / fireside / workshop, with target
  length

Example:

```
THE 7-TOUCH CADENCE
A keynote on why most B2B follow-up sequences die at touch 2 —
and the math behind the cadence that recovers 25% of closed-lost.
   - Why "I'll think about it" is statistically a 'yes' if you
     follow up correctly
   - The voicemail double-tap that drives email replies
   - The "second-no" play that reactivates 12% of dead deals
Format: 30-45 min keynote, or 90 min workshop

[Repeat for 2-4 more talks]
```

### Zone 3 — The credibility strip (next 15% of page)

- **Where you've spoken** — 3-7 named events / podcasts. If you're
  at the start of this motion and don't have a list yet, swap this
  zone for "Where my work has appeared" — publications you've been
  written about in, or where your essays have been republished.
- **Notable client logos** — 4-8 logos, only with permission.
- **One-line headline metric** — e.g., "200+ engagements across
  SaaS, services, and ecom."

### Zone 4 — The testimonial (next 10% of page)

ONE testimonial. Not five. One.

- 2-3 sentences max.
- From a name + company that the reader is likely to recognize OR
  whose title (e.g., "VP Sales, [Industry] Co.") gives credibility.
- Headshot of the person if possible.

If you have multiple equally strong testimonials, rotate them by
audience type — keep different variants of the one-pager for
different event categories.

### Zone 5 — The logistics block (next 15% of page)

The practical info an event organizer needs to make a decision:

- **Speaking fee:** [your rate, or "available on request" if you
  prefer to negotiate]
- **Travel:** "Available worldwide; coach for <6hr flights, business
  for longer."
- **Tech requirements:** "Wired mic preferred, slide remote, HDMI."
- **Lead time:** "Booking 60+ days out preferred."
- **Cancellation policy:** "[Your standard terms]"

This zone is where amateur one-pagers fall short. Including it
signals you've done this enough times to have a process.

### Zone 6 — The CTA + contact (bottom 10% of page)

- **Email** (a real human one, not info@)
- **Direct phone** (optional but powerful)
- **Calendar link** for a 20-min discovery call
- **Personal website** URL
- **Speaker reel** (a 60-second video clip of you on stage if
  you have one — even a clip from a webinar works for v1)

## The design rules

1. **Hire a designer.** $300-$1500 once. Worth it. A homemade Canva
   one-pager screams amateur. Find a designer who specifically does
   speaker / executive personal-brand work.
2. **Use the same fonts + colors as your website / book / brand.**
   Consistency reads as established.
3. **One color accent, max.** A second color is one too many on a
   one-page PDF.
4. **Generous white space.** A crammed one-pager doesn't get read.
   Cut content if it doesn't fit comfortably with whitespace.
5. **PDF, not Google Doc or web page.** Event organizers want
   something they can save and forward. PDF is the universal
   format.

## What goes IN every version

Universal across audience types:
- Your name + positioning + headshot
- Your contact + CTA
- One testimonial (rotate by audience)

## What VARIES by version

Build 2-3 versions for different audiences if your work spans
multiple categories. Each version:
- Different signature talks (lead with the most relevant)
- Different credibility list (lead with relevant events)
- Different testimonial (one whose title matches the audience)

Example: a SaaS-conference version of the one-pager leads with the
SaaS testimonial and the SaaS-relevant talks; a services-industry
version leads with the services testimonial.

## How to use it

1. **Pitch follow-up.** When you pitch a podcast or speaking gig,
   attach this. Don't make them ask.
2. **Cold opener** — when a former colleague refers you, send to
   the organizer immediately with a 2-sentence intro.
3. **Bio request** — when a host asks "send me your bio + headshot,"
   send the full one-pager. They'll be relieved.
4. **Website / "Speaking" page** — embed the PDF preview + offer it
   as a download.
5. **Email signature** — link in your signature for 30 days after
   each speaking gig.

## The maintenance ritual

Update every 90 days:
- Refresh the credibility list (add new speaking gigs).
- Rotate the testimonial if you have a fresher one.
- Update the rate if you've raised.
- Refresh the headshot every 18-24 months (people change; sync
  the photo to current you).

A stale one-pager from 2 years ago undermines everything else. The
ritual is worth the 30 minutes.

## What kills speaker one-pagers

1. **Too much text.** If a reader has to scroll-zoom on a phone, the
   page is too dense.
2. **No specific talks.** "Available to speak on a variety of
   topics" → unbookable. Name your talks.
3. **No headshot.** Removes the personal hook. Even a decent iPhone
   photo beats no photo.
4. **No contact info or CTA.** Sounds obvious. Easy to forget.
5. **Building it once and never updating.** Stale signals "this
   person isn't actively in the speaking circuit."
