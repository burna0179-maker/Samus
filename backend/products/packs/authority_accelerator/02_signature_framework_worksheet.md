# Signature Framework Worksheet

A half-day exercise that produces the keystone deliverable of the
authority-building motion: **the named framework that becomes
synonymous with you in your niche**.

## Why a named framework matters

People don't remember "that person who does workflow consulting."
They remember "the FALCON method person." They remember "the woman
who invented the 5-stage onboarding map."

A named framework does four things:

1. **Aids recall.** A name is sticky in a way that descriptions aren't.
2. **Creates ownership.** When the framework name Googles to you, you
   own the topic.
3. **Compounds in conversation.** Clients, podcasts, articles can
   reference it by name — citing you each time without explanation.
4. **Becomes a teaching tool.** A framework with 3-7 named stages is
   far easier to teach in a 60-minute keynote than a flat list of
   "best practices."

## The 6-step exercise

Block 4 hours. Phone off. One sitting if possible.

### Step 1 — Inventory the work you do (30 min)

List, in plain English, every kind of work you actually do for
clients. Be specific. Not "marketing strategy" — "I help SaaS
founders pick which channel to invest their first $50k of growth
budget in" or "I install structured 7-touch follow-up cadences in
B2B sales orgs."

Aim for 5-15 specific work descriptions.

### Step 2 — Find the through-line (45 min)

Look at your list. What's the pattern? What kind of *problem* are
you actually solving in 80% of these engagements?

The trick: zoom out one level of abstraction. If your work is
mostly "follow-up cadences" + "outbound sequences" + "discovery
call prep," the through-line might be "**unforced revenue leakage**
in the post-quote phase."

Write the through-line as one sentence. Refine until it feels right.
A good through-line:
- Names a *problem* (not your solution).
- Is specific enough to be ownable but general enough to cover 80%
  of your work.
- Sounds like something a smart client would actually say to a peer
  about why they hired you.

### Step 3 — Articulate the 3-7 stages of how you solve it (60 min)

How do you actually solve the through-line problem, broken into
discrete steps? List 3-7 stages. Each stage:
- Is a verb phrase (not a noun).
- Has a clear deliverable / output.
- Can be checked: "did this stage happen yet?"

Example for "unforced revenue leakage in the post-quote phase":

1. **Audit** the current post-quote behavior (what touches are
   actually happening?)
2. **Map** the leakage points (where do deals actually die?)
3. **Design** the 7-touch cadence + objection branches
4. **Install** the cadence into your tools + train you on running it
5. **Tune** based on the first 30 days of data
6. **Hand off** to operator-owned ritual

That's 6 stages. Each is a verb. Each has clear outputs.

### Step 4 — Find the framework's name (60 min)

Brainstorm names. Aim for 30+ candidates before evaluating any.
Don't filter while brainstorming.

Once you have 30+, evaluate against this rubric:

| Criterion | Description | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Short** | 1-4 words | Can you say it in <2 seconds? |
| **Memorable** | Easy to recall after one exposure | Try it on 3 friends; ask them an hour later |
| **Searchable** | Not already owned by another framework | Google it; if page 1 is dominated by existing meaning, skip |
| **Implies the how** | Hints at your approach, not just the problem | "The [Name] Method" should feel descriptive, not arbitrary |
| **Pronounceable** | Easy to say in conversation | If you stumble saying it, it's wrong |

Common patterns that work:

- **Acronyms from your stage names** — e.g., AMDITH from
  Audit/Map/Design/Install/Tune/Hand-off. Often clunky; only use if
  it spells something memorable.
- **Verb-noun compounds** — e.g., "Revenue Plumbing," "Pipeline
  Forensics," "Cadence Engineering."
- **Industry-borrowed metaphors** — e.g., "The Trap-and-Release
  Method," "Pipeline MRI."
- **Eponymous** — e.g., "The Hartman Cadence" — works only if your
  name is already known in the niche. Otherwise feels presumptuous.

Pick your top 3. Sleep on it. Pick the final one in the morning.

### Step 5 — Write the framework's launch description (45 min)

Draft a 150-200 word description that you can use in:
- Your bio
- The opening of your launch essay (Q2 keystone)
- Your speaker one-pager
- The "about" section of any podcast you appear on

Template:

```
[Framework Name] is a [N]-stage approach to [the through-line
problem from Step 2]. It was developed across [N] client
engagements between [year] and [year], and it focuses on
[1-sentence on the unique angle].

The stages are: [Stage 1], [Stage 2], [Stage 3], …

The most common misconception about [Framework Name] is
[misconception]. The reason it works in practice — when most
[generic alternative] doesn't — is [the specific design choice].
```

### Step 6 — Validate with 3 trusted operators (30 min — async)

Send the launch description to 3 people in your niche whose
judgment you trust. Ask one specific question:

> "Honest take — does this feel like a real framework or like
> packaging? And if it's real, would you remember the name in a
> week?"

If 2/3 say "real and memorable," ship it. If 2/3 say "packaging,"
go back to Step 2 — the through-line wasn't sharp enough.

## After the worksheet

The framework is now ready to anchor your Q2 editorial plan (see
`01_twelve_month_editorial_plan.md`). Specifically:

- Q2 keystone essay = "Introducing [Framework Name]"
- Q2 short-form = one post per stage, plus design-rationale posts
- Q2 long-form = deep-dive per stage

## Common pitfalls

1. **Naming before defining.** Tempting to pick a name first and
   reverse-engineer the framework. Doesn't work — the name doesn't
   stick because it doesn't refer to anything specific.
2. **Too many stages.** 8+ stages is a process diagram, not a
   framework. People can hold 3-7 things in working memory.
3. **Generic name.** "The Growth Method" / "The Systems Approach"
   — already taken by 50 other consultants. Specificity is
   ownability.
4. **Renaming after launch.** Once you've shipped the launch essay,
   the name is locked in for at least 18 months. Pick carefully.
   Iterate the *content* of the framework, not the name.
5. **Framework without a problem.** A framework that doesn't have
   a clear through-line problem becomes an empty taxonomy.
