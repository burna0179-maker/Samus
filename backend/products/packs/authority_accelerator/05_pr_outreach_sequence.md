# PR Outreach Sequence

5-touch sequence for landing press coverage, bylined articles, and
industry-publication features. Designed for the operator who isn't
hiring a PR agency but wants to do this themselves on the side.

## The principle: journalists want stories, not heroes

Most cold PR pitches are "I'm great, please write about me." These
get deleted unread. The ones that get responses pitch a **story the
journalist's audience wants to read**, with you as a credible
source.

Reframe everything below through that lens.

## Choose your target type

There are three kinds of PR-adjacent placements. Each has a
different sequence.

### Type 1 — Bylined article (you write, they publish)

Publications like industry trade journals, professional society
magazines, and most business blogs accept bylined contributions.
Hit rate at typical operator stage: 25-40% acceptance — much
higher than press coverage because you're saving them work.

### Type 2 — Quoted source (they write, you're cited)

Reporters at business publications quote sources in their stories.
Hit rate: 5-10% acceptance per pitch, but each landed citation is
valuable forever.

### Type 3 — Profile / feature (they write about you)

The hardest to land but the most powerful. Hit rate: <2% from cold,
typically requires a hook (a launch, a milestone, a controversy, a
data report).

## The 5-touch sequence (per publication)

Run separately per publication. Don't multi-thread the same pitch
across many.

### Touch 1 — Research first (Day 0, no contact yet)

Spend 30 min reading the publication. Identify:
- The 2-3 journalists who cover your topic area.
- The specific kinds of stories they write (length, tone, angle).
- A recent article you can credibly reference in your pitch.

### Touch 2 — The cold pitch (Day 1)

Email length: under 200 words. Subject specific.

**For Type 1 (bylined article) — Subject: `[Topic angle] — pitch for [pub name]`**

```
Hey [reporter first name],

I read your piece on [recent article topic] — [specific detail
that resonated]. I have a related angle I'd like to pitch as a
contributed piece for [pub name]:

WORKING TITLE: [Specific outcome-focused title]

THE ANGLE: [2-3 sentences on what's new about this take and why
your audience needs it now]

WHY ME: [One sentence on your specific credibility — "I've run
this approach across [N] companies / wrote about it in [other pub] /
own the [framework name] methodology"]

LENGTH I'M PROPOSING: [800-1500 words, or whatever matches the
publication's typical length]

DELIVERY: I can have a draft in your inbox within [N] days of green
light.

Happy to share an outline before I write the full piece if that
helps.

— [Your name]
[Your one-pager link]
```

**For Type 2 (quoted source) — Subject: `Source for your [topic] coverage`**

```
Hey [reporter first name],

Saw your piece on [recent article]. If you're continuing to cover
[topic area], I might be useful as a source.

SHORT VERSION: I work with [audience] on [specific problem]. The
counterintuitive thing I keep seeing — that I haven't seen
referenced in [pub name] yet — is [specific insight, in one
sentence].

I can share specific data, anonymized client examples, or a
particularly weird-but-true case if any of those would feed a
future piece. Always happy to do a quick call.

— [Your name]
[Your one-pager link]
[Direct phone]
```

**For Type 3 (profile) — Subject: `Story idea: [hook]`**

```
Hey [reporter first name],

Long shot, but here goes. I'm pitching a story idea, not asking to
be featured personally.

THE STORY: [The narrative arc — 3-4 sentences. Must have a hook:
a milestone, a contrarian position, a launch, a data report.]

WHY NOW: [The timeliness — why this story is interesting THIS
quarter, not just generally]

ANGLES YOU COULD TAKE: [2-3 different framings the reporter could
use]

WHAT I CAN PROVIDE: [Data, interview access, anonymized client
stories, on-the-record quotes]

I'm the source for it, but I'm not the only source — happy to
point you at 3-4 other operators with related perspectives if you
want to triangulate.

— [Your name]
[Your one-pager link]
```

### Touch 3 — The bump (Day 8)

No response after a week is normal. Bump:

```
Hey [first name],

Quick bump on the [topic] pitch from last week — wanted to make
sure it didn't get lost.

Happy to send 2 alternative angles if the original isn't right
for you.

— [Your name]
```

### Touch 4 — The value-add (Day 22)

Two weeks after the bump. Don't pitch — be useful:

```
Hey [first name],

Saw [a relevant article / data point / event] and thought of you.
[1 sentence on the specific thing and why it might be useful for
your coverage area.]

No agenda — just thought you'd find it interesting.

— [Your name]
```

### Touch 5 — The polite retire (Day 60)

After two months of no engagement:

```
Hey [first name],

Closing the loop on the [topic] pitch from earlier this year.
Totally understand if it wasn't the right fit.

I'll stop pestering. If you ever need a source on [your topic
area] in the future, [pub name] readers seem to be exactly the
audience my work resonates with — please keep me in mind.

— [Your name]
```

After Touch 5, retire the pitch. Reapproach the same reporter
12+ months later, with a different angle.

## What to pitch when

| Quarter | Pitch types |
|---|---|
| **Q1** | Type 1 (bylined). Build the publication relationship. |
| **Q2** | Type 2 (source). Signal expertise on your signature framework. |
| **Q3** | Type 2 again — case-study-driven. |
| **Q4** | Type 3 (profile). Use your year-end keystone essay as the hook. |

This sequencing matters — most reporters won't profile you until
they've seen you cited 3-5 times in adjacent coverage. Type 2
builds the credibility for Type 3 to succeed.

## How to handle a "yes"

When a reporter responds positively:

1. **Reply within 4 hours.** Speed signals professionalism.
2. **Confirm scope** — length, deadline, format, any payment.
3. **Deliver early** — at least 24 hours before deadline.
4. **Follow editorial guidance** — if they ask for edits, do them
   fast and without arguing.
5. **Promote the piece** when it runs — share on your channels,
   tag the publication + reporter, send a thank-you note.

## The big-bang option

If you don't have time for sustained PR outreach, build ONE
**big-bang asset** per year:
- An original data report (e.g., "We surveyed 200 SaaS founders…")
- A book launch
- A controversial position essay
- A redesigned-from-scratch product / service launch

Then pitch that single asset to 20-50 publications in a
concentrated 30-day window. Hit rate is much higher because
you're not asking "cover me," you're saying "cover this thing
that just happened."

## What kills PR outreach

1. **Pitching the wrong reporter.** A tech reporter doesn't cover
   personal finance. Read 3 of their recent pieces before pitching.
2. **No specific angle.** "I'd love to be quoted in [pub]" — about
   what? Pitch a story.
3. **Press release format.** Reporters hate press releases.
   Write a personal email.
4. **No follow-through after a yes.** Missing a deadline burns the
   reporter forever.
5. **Treating PR as one-shot.** Coverage compounds — getting cited
   in [pub] makes the next pitch easier. PR is a 12-month motion,
   minimum.
