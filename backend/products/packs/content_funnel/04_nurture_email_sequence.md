# 7-Email Nurture Sequence — MOFU Lead Warming

A 21-day email sequence that takes a fresh lead-magnet signup from
"downloaded a thing" to "ready to consider buying." Email service
provider-agnostic — wire this into ConvertKit, MailerLite, Beehiiv,
Postmark, or whatever you use.

## The principle

Most nurture sequences fail because they pivot to "buy from me" by
email 3. The reader's defenses go up and the rest of the sequence is
ignored.

The correct pacing: emails 1-5 build trust by giving value, email 6
introduces the offer softly, email 7 invites direct conversation.

## Sequence overview

| Email | Day | Goal | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1 | 0 (immediate) | Deliver the magnet + set tone | 80-150 words |
| #2 | 2 | Show you understand their problem deeply | 200-300 words |
| #3 | 5 | Teach one specific tactic they can use today | 250-400 words |
| #4 | 9 | Share a story (success or failure) | 250-400 words |
| #5 | 13 | Surface a contrarian take that demonstrates expertise | 250-400 words |
| #6 | 17 | Introduce the paid offer softly | 200-300 words |
| #7 | 21 | Direct invitation to engage | 150-250 words |

## The 7 emails

### Email #1 — Delivery (Day 0)

Subject: `Your [lead magnet name] (and one note from me)`

```
Hey [first name],

Here's the [lead magnet name] you asked for: [LINK / ATTACHMENT]

Two quick notes:

1. The best way to get value from this is to actually open it within
   24 hours. The longer you wait, the more it gets buried under the
   rest of your week.

2. I'll be sending you 6 more emails over the next 3 weeks. Each one
   is short, specific, and useful. If they're not, hit reply and tell
   me — I read every reply.

That's it. Go open the [magnet name].

— [Your name]

P.S. If you have any questions about [topic the magnet covers], just
reply to this email. I'm a human, not an autoresponder.
```

### Email #2 — Deep diagnosis (Day 2)

Subject: `The hidden reason [problem the magnet addresses] keeps recurring`

```
Hey [first name],

You downloaded the [magnet name] because [their inferred reason —
"you're tired of X" / "you've tried Y and it didn't stick" / etc.].

Here's the thing most people miss about that problem: it's not really
about [the obvious cause]. It's about [the underlying mechanism].

When I worked with [a specific example — anonymized if needed], they
had the exact same pattern. They'd tried [common solution attempt]
three times. Each time, it stuck for 3-4 weeks and then quietly
unraveled. The reason wasn't lack of discipline. It was that
[mechanism — the structural thing they hadn't noticed].

Once they fixed the structural thing, [specific outcome happened] —
and it stayed fixed.

If this is feeling familiar, that's not an accident. It's the
default failure mode for [your audience].

I'll send a tactical post tomorrow with the first step you can take
this week. Until then: did anything in the [magnet name] land hard?
Hit reply and tell me which part.

— [Your name]
```

### Email #3 — Tactical (Day 5)

Subject: `One thing to try this week`

```
Hey [first name],

Specific tactic for you this week.

[Name the tactic. One sentence.]

Here's how to do it in under 30 minutes:

1. [Step 1, specific action]
2. [Step 2, specific action]
3. [Step 3, specific action]

When you're done, you'll have [specific output]. The point of having
[specific output] is that it lets you [specific outcome].

If you do it this week, I'd love to hear what came out of it. Hit
reply with any one bullet point from your output and I'll respond
with a quick reaction.

— [Your name]

P.S. If you've already done something like this, double down on
[specific advanced variation]. There's an extra layer most people
don't notice.
```

### Email #4 — Story (Day 9)

Subject: `The [size] mistake I made on [topic]`

```
Hey [first name],

Story time. Earlier this year I made a [size — "$15k" / "6-month" /
"painful" / etc.] mistake on [topic the magnet covers].

[2-3 short paragraphs telling the story. Concrete. Specific dates,
numbers, real consequences. No hedging. No "and the lesson is" —
let the story breathe.]

What I'd do differently if I were starting from scratch:

1. [Specific action 1]
2. [Specific action 2]
3. [Specific action 3]

The reason I'm sharing this isn't because I figured something out
that nobody else has. It's because most people who write about
[topic] only share the wins. The losses are where the real lessons
are.

If you've made a similar mistake, you're not alone. Hit reply and
tell me about it — I keep a private collection of these so I can
write about the patterns later.

— [Your name]
```

### Email #5 — Contrarian (Day 13)

Subject: `Why [common advice] is wrong (for most people)`

```
Hey [first name],

Quick take. Everyone in [your industry / domain] keeps repeating
this advice:

> "[The common advice, verbatim or close to it.]"

I think it's wrong for most people most of the time. Here's why:

[3-4 paragraphs of the actual argument. Use real evidence — numbers,
examples, named cases. Acknowledge the steel-man version of the
opposing view; don't strawman.]

The version of the advice that IS true for most people is:

> "[Your refined / corrected version of the advice.]"

The reason the original advice gets passed around anyway: it's
shorter and it makes for better headlines. Reality is messier.

If your situation is one of the edge cases where the original advice
DOES apply, you'll know — it'll feel right because the conditions
match. If it doesn't feel right, trust that signal.

— [Your name]
```

### Email #6 — Soft offer (Day 17)

Subject: `Something I built for [audience]`

```
Hey [first name],

Quick note. I've been hesitant to mention this because I'm not great
at "selling" — but here's what I work on, in case it's relevant.

I help [exact audience] with [exact outcome]. The way it works is:
[1-2 sentences on the format / process].

Common reasons people work with me:
- [Specific use case 1]
- [Specific use case 2]
- [Specific use case 3]

Common reasons people don't:
- [Specific honest disqualifier 1]
- [Specific honest disqualifier 2]

Pricing starts at [$X — be specific]. There's a [free conversation /
discovery call / no-commitment audit] you can book here:
[LINK].

If now's not the right time, no problem. The rest of the sequence
(one more email after this one) doesn't pitch anything — it's just a
direct invitation to keep the conversation going.

— [Your name]
```

### Email #7 — Direct invitation (Day 21)

Subject: `Last email — and a question`

```
Hey [first name],

This is the last email in this sequence. Three weeks ago you
downloaded the [magnet name]. Hopefully you got something useful out
of it.

Before I let you go from this nurture (you'll still hear from me in
my regular newsletter, but the sequenced emails stop here), one
direct ask:

What's the single biggest thing you're trying to figure out about
[topic] right now? Reply with one sentence. I read everything and I'll
respond.

If I can answer your question in an email, I will. If it needs a real
conversation, I'll suggest one. If it's outside what I work on, I'll
point you toward someone who can help.

Whichever way it goes, thanks for being here.

— [Your name]

P.S. If you'd rather just stay on the newsletter and skip the rest,
no reply needed. I'll see you in your inbox.
```

## Wiring it up (ESP-agnostic)

Most email service providers support the basics:

1. **Trigger:** Form submission OR new subscriber to the
   "lead-magnet" tag.
2. **Sequence:** 7 emails on the schedule above (immediate, +2 days,
   +5 days, +9 days, +13 days, +17 days, +21 days).
3. **Exit condition:** Either (a) reaches email 7, or (b) replies
   to any email (move to "engaged" segment), or (c) clicks the
   email-6 CTA (move to "interested" segment).

ConvertKit, MailerLite, Beehiiv all support this with their
visual-automation builders. Postmark / SendGrid / SES need you to
write the logic in your own app — usually a small Python script
that runs daily and dispatches due emails.

## What kills nurture sequences

1. **Pitching too early.** If email 1 is "buy from me," delete this
   sequence and start over.
2. **All same-format emails.** Variety matters. Tactical / story /
   contrarian / offer — different shapes hold attention.
3. **No reply solicitation.** Every email should have a "hit reply"
   prompt somewhere. Replies are the gold — they tag your engaged
   leads.
4. **Length inflation.** 800-word emails get skimmed. 250-word
   emails get read.
5. **No exit.** If a reader engages or buys, they shouldn't
   continue getting nurture emails. Set up the segment moves.
