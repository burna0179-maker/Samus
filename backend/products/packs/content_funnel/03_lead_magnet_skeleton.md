# Lead Magnet Skeleton — TOFU → MOFU Conversion

The lead magnet is the asset that converts a casual reader into a
named email address. Once they're on your list, you control the
relationship instead of renting it from a platform.

## What makes a lead magnet actually work

The single biggest mistake is treating the lead magnet as a
"thank-you gift" — a free thing in exchange for an email. That framing
guarantees the magnet underperforms.

The correct framing: the lead magnet is **a high-quality miniature of
your paid offer**. Same problem, smaller scope, fully delivered. The
reader who downloads it should think "if the free thing is this good,
the paid thing must be excellent."

## The 5 lead-magnet patterns that work

### 1. The checklist (best for: action-oriented audiences)

A 1-2 page printable list of "do this, then this, then this." Works
when your audience is mid-implementation on something specific.

**Examples:**
- "Stripe webhook hardening checklist — the 11 boxes to tick before
  going live"
- "Pre-sales-call checklist — the 7 questions to ask before any
  discovery call"

### 2. The diagnostic / audit (best for: research-stage audiences)

A self-administered 10-20 question quiz that gives the reader a
diagnosis (with a recommended next action).

**Examples:**
- "Are your automations costing you money? Take the 8-question audit"
- "What stage of operator are you? 12 questions to find out"

### 3. The framework / template (best for: builder audiences)

A reusable structure the reader can fill in for their own situation.
A Notion template, a Google Sheet, an Airtable base, a markdown
outline.

**Examples:**
- "The 4-week content calendar template (Notion + Google Sheet
  versions)"
- "The 7-email follow-up sequence — fill in your offer, deploy in an
  hour"

### 4. The mini-course (best for: deep-context audiences)

A 3-5 email sequence (or short video series) that teaches one
specific skill. Best for when the topic genuinely needs sequencing
to land.

**Examples:**
- "5 days, 5 emails: how to design a discovery call that books
  itself"
- "The 7-day prompt engineering bootcamp for non-engineers"

### 5. The data report (best for: insider audiences)

You compile a small original dataset — 50 interviews, 100 company
reviews, a tools-stack survey — and write up the findings.

**Examples:**
- "How 87 SaaS founders priced their first 10 customers (anonymized)"
- "The 2026 solo-consultant tech stack — survey of 134 practitioners"

## How to choose your pattern

Match the pattern to where your audience is in their journey:

| Audience state | Best pattern |
|---|---|
| Knows the problem, ready to act | Checklist or template |
| Vaguely concerned, not sure if applies | Diagnostic |
| Wants to learn the topic | Mini-course |
| Wants to feel insider/in-the-know | Data report |
| Wants the SHORTCUT, not understanding | Template |

For most operators starting from scratch: **build the template or the
checklist**. They're the fastest to ship and the easiest to validate.

## The 6-hour build process

You can build a credible v1 lead magnet in one focused 6-hour day.
The breakdown:

### Hour 1 — Decide

- Pick the pattern from the table above.
- Write one sentence: "After someone downloads this, they will be
  able to [specific outcome]."
- Write the title — short, specific, outcome-focused. Test the title
  against this rule: "Would I download this if I saw it as a CTA?"
  If no, rewrite.

### Hours 2-4 — Build

- Open a fresh doc. Write the asset.
- Keep it tight. A 6-page PDF beats a 40-page ebook for conversion
  almost every time. Length signals "we ran out of editing energy,"
  not "we're more thorough."
- Use a real example throughout. Not "Acme Co" — a real or
  realistically-named example with real numbers.

### Hour 5 — Polish

- Format. Add headings. Use bullets where they read better than
  paragraphs.
- Add a cover page with: title, your name, your URL, your CTA.
- Add a final page with: "what's next" — a clear bridge from the
  lead magnet to your paid offer or your nurture sequence.
- Export to PDF.

### Hour 6 — Wire up

- Create the landing page (Carrd, ConvertKit landing pages, a Notion
  page with a form embed — anything that works).
- Set up the email automation: signup → send PDF → enter nurture
  sequence (see `04_nurture_email_sequence.md`).
- Test it end to end with a personal email.

## The lead-magnet landing page

The landing page should fit on one screen with no scroll required.
The structure:

```
[Headline — the outcome they get, in one line]

[Subheadline — one sentence on what's in it and who it's for]

[Form field: Email]
[Submit button: "Send it to me"]

[3-4 bullets of what's inside]

[One-line credibility statement — who you are]
```

That's it. No testimonials yet (you don't have them at v1). No
20-bullet feature list. The page should take 12 seconds to read and
3 seconds to decide.

## Distribution — where the lead magnet lives

After you ship the v1, make sure it appears in 5 places:

1. **Bio links** — Twitter, LinkedIn, Instagram, your site.
2. **Pinned post** on your primary platform.
3. **Bottom of every long-form piece** — a soft CTA at the end:
   "If this resonated, here's a [magnet name] that goes deeper."
4. **Sales call follow-up** — when a discovery call doesn't close
   immediately, send the magnet as part of the polite-no-touch.
5. **Cold outbound** — send the magnet as the second touch in any
   cold outreach sequence (after the personalized intro).

## Validation — is your magnet working?

Track three numbers, weekly:

1. **Landing-page visit → signup rate.** Target: 25%+ for warm
   traffic, 8%+ for cold. Below 8%, the landing page or magnet
   promise is off.
2. **Open rate of the delivery email.** Target: 70%+. If it's lower,
   your subject line is failing.
3. **Nurture sequence engagement rate** (% who open at least 3 of
   the 7 emails). Target: 40%+. If lower, your nurture content
   isn't matching the magnet's promise.

If any number is below target for 4 weeks, fix that specific stage
before optimizing anything else.

## What kills lead magnets

1. **Length-as-a-virtue.** A 50-page PDF that nobody reads is worse
   than a 6-page checklist that everyone uses.
2. **Generic title.** "The Ultimate Guide to Marketing" loses to
   "The 8-question pre-call audit that lifted my close rate from
   18% to 47%."
3. **No bridge to next action.** Reader gets the magnet, says
   "thanks," leaves. The last page MUST point at what's next.
4. **Hidden behind a 14-field form.** Email only. That's the only
   field that matters at v1.
