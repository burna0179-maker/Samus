# Short-Form Hooks — TOFU Top Layer

Short-form (LinkedIn / X / Threads / Bluesky / TikTok captions) hooks
designed to earn the first impression from people who don't yet know
they have the problem your offer solves.

## The structural rule

Every short-form post needs three things in three lines:

1. **The hook** — earn the click/expand.
2. **The proof** — give one specific detail that makes the hook
   credible.
3. **The bridge** — point at what the reader gets if they keep going.

After that you have ~6-15 sentences to deliver the actual value, then
ONE clear CTA.

## Hook patterns that work for problem-unaware audiences

### 1. The hidden-cost hook

> [Familiar activity] is costing you [counterintuitive amount]. Here's
> the math.

**Why it works:** Forces the reader to mentally check whether the
claim applies to them. If they can't immediately disprove it, they
keep reading.

**Example:**
> Your inbox is costing you 2 hours a day. Here's the math:
> - Average context-switch recovery: 23 minutes
> - Average inbox checks per day: 18
> - 18 × ~7 min lost focus per check = 2 hours, gone
> Most operators don't see this because the time gets lost in
> minutes, not blocks.

---

### 2. The misdiagnosis hook

> If you think your problem is [obvious symptom], you might actually
> have [underlying cause].

**Why it works:** Reframes a known frustration. Reader thinks "wait,
is THAT what's actually going on?"

**Example:**
> If you think your problem is "I don't have enough time," you might
> actually have "I never decided what I'd say no to." The first is
> permanent. The second is solvable in an afternoon.

---

### 3. The category-clarifier hook

> There are 3 kinds of [thing]. Most people are doing #2 and wondering
> why it isn't working. Here's how to tell them apart.

**Why it works:** Makes the reader curious which category they're in.
Implies you have a vocabulary they don't.

**Example:**
> There are 3 kinds of LinkedIn posts: broadcast, conversation, and
> sales. Most creators are writing broadcast posts and wondering why
> nobody DMs them. Here's how to tell them apart.

---

### 4. The "what you should be measuring" hook

> Stop measuring [common vanity metric]. Start measuring [better
> metric]. Here's why one is signal and the other is noise.

**Why it works:** Promises a more useful mental model. Casts doubt on
the reader's current dashboard.

**Example:**
> Stop measuring "followers." Start measuring "DMs from people who
> aren't bots." Followers are vanity. DMs are pipeline.

---

### 5. The expensive-mistake hook

> The most expensive mistake [audience] makes is [specific mistake].
> I've made it. Here's how to avoid it.

**Why it works:** Pain framing + first-person credibility. Reader
defaults to "let me see if I'm making this too."

**Example:**
> The most expensive mistake solo operators make is hiring before
> they've systematized their own work. I've made it. Here's how to
> avoid it.

---

### 6. The unspoken-rule hook

> Nobody talks about [unspoken-but-real thing]. So I will.

**Why it works:** Implies insider information. Reader expects to learn
something they couldn't Google.

**Example:**
> Nobody talks about the loneliness of running a service business
> after your first year. So I will.

---

### 7. The "I tracked it" hook

> I tracked [activity] for [N days/weeks]. The results surprised me.

**Why it works:** Self-experiment framing — credible, specific,
inherently curiosity-inducing.

**Example:**
> I tracked every hour of my "work" for 30 days. I was actually
> producing for 11 hours a week. The other 30 were context-switching,
> meetings, and email. Here's what I changed.

---

### 8. The decision-framework hook

> Here's how I decide [common decision] in under 60 seconds.

**Why it works:** Promises a tool the reader can use immediately.
Specific time-boxing makes it feel feasible.

**Example:**
> Here's how I decide whether to take a sales call in under 60
> seconds: I ask 2 questions in the booking form. If both are red,
> I pass.

---

## The CTA bank — pick one per post

Short-form posts die from "no clear next action." Use one of these,
verbatim or close to it:

- **Reply CTA:** "What's your take on this? Curious if others have
  hit the same thing."
- **DM CTA:** "If this resonates, DM me with [specific question] —
  happy to compare notes."
- **Share CTA:** "If you know an operator who needs to read this,
  share it with them."
- **Lead-magnet CTA:** "I've put together a [lead magnet] on this.
  Link in bio." (Use sparingly — feels promotional fast.)
- **Implicit CTA:** End on a strong line, full stop. Sometimes the
  best CTA is no CTA, especially for posts in the "story" register.

## Posting cadence

5 short-form posts per week is the sustainable sweet spot for one
human:
- Mon/Wed/Fri: pattern-interrupt hooks (1, 4, 7 above)
- Tue/Thu: relationship hooks (2, 5, 6 above)

Use pattern 3 and 8 monthly — they're framework-heavy and grow stale
if repeated weekly.

## What kills short-form

1. **Throat-clearing.** "So I was thinking…" Cut everything before
   the actual hook.
2. **Multiple ideas.** One post = one idea. Split if necessary.
3. **No CTA.** A post without a next action is content marketing
   theater.
4. **Vague specifics.** "Many people" / "I've seen this a lot" —
   replace with real numbers, real names, real dates.
