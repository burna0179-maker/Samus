# Sales Follow-Up Workflow Playbook

**Audience:** Operators and sales practitioners who have a pipeline of
quoted-but-not-yet-closed deals that quietly die because nobody
followed up at the right time, in the right channel, with the right
message.

**What this playbook installs:** A 7-touch follow-up cadence over 21
days that converts more "thinking about it" into "yes," with a
disciplined escalation to the "second-no" reactivation play when the
deal cools — and a permanent retire rule so you don't haunt prospects
who clearly aren't buying.

---

## §1. The problem this solves

Most deals don't die because the prospect chose a competitor. Most
deals die because:

- The prospect got distracted by their own job.
- The decision-maker went on vacation / changed roles / got sick.
- An internal stakeholder you didn't know existed put up a hand.
- The follow-up arrived once on day 3, then never again.

The single most expensive mistake in sales is the deal you let go
silent. The conversion math is brutal: a deal you've quoted and then
abandoned converts at roughly 0%. A deal you've quoted and followed
up consistently through a structured cadence converts at 15-35%
depending on industry. Your follow-up discipline is the difference.

This isn't about being pushy. It's about being **predictable, useful,
and persistent in the same way a good professional service is**.

## §2. The diagnostic — what's actually happening in your follow-up today

Pull the last 30 closed-lost deals from your CRM (or pipeline tracker,
or memory if there's no system yet). For each, capture:

| Question | Capture |
|---|---|
| How many follow-up touches did you actually send after the quote? | Number |
| What channel(s) — email only, phone, LinkedIn, SMS? | List |
| What was the final touch? | Free text |
| Did the prospect ever explicitly say "no"? | Y/N |
| Best guess: would they have closed with one more well-timed touch? | Y/N/Maybe |

Two numbers come out of this:

1. **Median touch count per closed-lost deal.** Industry-typical bad
   number: 1.5. Industry-typical good number: 5+. If you're below 3,
   you're throwing away deals.
2. **% of closed-lost deals where the prospect never said no.** If
   this number is above 50%, your problem isn't lost deals — it's
   stalled deals that you let die.

Both numbers are recoverable. This playbook is the recovery plan.

## §3. The 7-touch cadence

The cadence runs 21 days from the day you quoted. It assumes the
prospect has not yet either confirmed or hard-declined. Each touch
has a specific channel, a specific message shape, and a specific
purpose.

### Touch 1 — Quote follow-up (Day 1)

**Channel:** Email (same thread as the quote).

**Purpose:** Confirm receipt + set expectation for the cadence.

```
Hey [name],

Wanted to make sure the quote I sent yesterday landed cleanly. No
rush to decide — most folks take a few days. I'll check back in next
week if I haven't heard from you, and again the week after, then I'll
stop pestering you. Sound fair?

If you have any questions before then, just reply.

— [your name]
```

The "sound fair?" close is doing real work. It creates an explicit
expectation that you *will* follow up, which means each subsequent
touch is a promise kept rather than an annoyance.

### Touch 2 — Value-add nudge (Day 4)

**Channel:** Email (new thread).

**Purpose:** Be useful without asking for anything.

```
Subject: Quick thing that might be useful — [their topic]

Hey [name],

Saw [this article / this tool / this case study] and immediately
thought of what we talked about. Sharing in case it's useful:
[link or short summary]

No reply needed — just thought you'd find it interesting.

— [your name]
```

The discipline: this email **does not mention the quote**. It's a
relationship deposit. If you blend a "checking on the proposal" into
this email, you've burned the touch.

### Touch 3 — The check-in (Day 7)

**Channel:** Email (reply to the quote thread).

**Purpose:** Soft ask for status.

```
Hey [name],

Following the cadence I promised — wanted to check in on the proposal.

Honest question: is there anything that's making this a "not now"?
If timing's off, just let me know and I'll circle back next quarter.
If there's a specific question I can answer, hit reply and I'll get
back to you same-day.

— [your name]
```

The "honest question" frame is borrowed from the NEPQ school of
selling and it works because it's a permission slip for the prospect
to be candid without feeling guilty.

### Touch 4 — The voicemail double-tap (Day 10)

**Channel:** Phone. Two voicemails on the same day, ~3 hours apart,
ideally from two different numbers if you have them (per Nick Cegelski
of 30MPC's data-backed framework).

**Voicemail #1 (15 seconds — context only):**

```
Hey [name], it's [your name] calling about the [their topic / project]
quote we sent over. I'll shoot you an email too — just wanted to put
a voice to it. Talk soon.
```

**Voicemail #2 (30 seconds — social proof):**

```
Hey [name], me again — last call from me on the [their topic] piece.
We just wrapped a similar engagement with [comparable company /
situation] and the result was [one specific, credible number]. Figured
you'd want to know before deciding. Email me back if it changes your
read on it. Cheers.
```

The voicemail double-tap's goal is **NOT a callback** — it's to get
an email reply. Voicemails generate replies because they signal
seriousness without requiring a return phone commitment.

### Touch 5 — LinkedIn touch (Day 13)

**Channel:** LinkedIn message (if connected) or a comment on something
they've recently posted (if not connected).

**Purpose:** Channel diversification + softer-touch reminder.

If connected, send a DM:

```
Hey [name] — re-checking in on the [their topic] proposal. Also, this
post of yours about [their recent post] resonated — particularly the
point about [specific thing]. Curious how that's playing out in your
day-to-day.
```

If not connected, comment thoughtfully on a recent post they've made.
Don't request connection in the same beat — let the comment land
first, request connection 24 hours later.

### Touch 6 — The "second-no" preempt (Day 17)

**Channel:** Email.

**Purpose:** Surface the real objection so it can be addressed.

```
Hey [name],

I've sent a few touches and haven't heard back, which is fine — most
"no" decisions never get said out loud. Before I close the loop on
this one, just wanted to ask directly:

Is there a specific reason this isn't moving forward? It would help
me a lot — whether it's a budget mismatch, the timing, the scope, or
something about us specifically, I'd genuinely rather know than guess.

If the answer is "it's just not the right time," totally understood.
I'll bow out and circle back in 90 days.

— [your name]
```

This message is doing two things: (1) it gives the prospect a
psychologically easy way to say no without feeling rude, and (2) it
extracts the *real* objection in the cases where the deal could be
saved by addressing one specific concern.

### Touch 7 — The polite close (Day 21)

**Channel:** Email.

**Purpose:** End the cadence with dignity and leave the door open.

```
Hey [name],

Going to stop following up since I haven't heard back — totally fair,
I know how busy things get. The quote stays valid through [date 60
days out] if you ever want to pick it back up. If your situation
changes — for example if [specific trigger condition], please loop
back.

Best of luck with [their topic / project / quarter].

— [your name]
```

After Touch 7, the prospect goes into the long-term nurture (§5).
You do NOT keep adding touches. Discipline matters here.

## §4. Objection branches (when they DO reply)

If at any touch the prospect replies with an objection, branch
immediately out of the cadence and handle the objection. The cadence
resumes only if the objection is resolved and they go silent again.

The objection-to-script map for the most common five:

### "Too expensive"

> Totally get it. Before we close the loop on price, mind if I ask
> what you're comparing the cost against? Sometimes it's a competitor
> quote, sometimes it's the internal "if we just did this ourselves"
> number, sometimes it's both. The answer determines what we can do.

### "Bad timing"

> Fair. Two questions: (1) is timing the only thing, or is there also
> something else in play? (2) when does timing get better — is it a
> calendar thing (e.g. next quarter) or an event thing (e.g. after
> [specific milestone])? Either is fine to share.

### "Need to think about it"

> Understood. What specifically do you want to think through? I can
> usually answer in a sentence or two via email, and it saves you
> the brain cycles.

(Never ask the prospect to defend the objection by asking *"why?"* —
it hardens the position. Always offer to help with the thinking.)

### "Need to talk to my partner / boss"

> Makes sense. Anything I can put together to make that conversation
> easier? Most folks find a one-page summary of scope + cost + timeline
> works well — happy to put one together tailored to your partner's
> specific concerns if you let me know what they tend to focus on.

### "We decided to go with [competitor]"

> Congrats on making a decision — that's the hardest part. One ask:
> mind sharing what tipped the scale? It helps me a ton on future
> engagements, and I'll keep you posted if our offering changes in a
> way that might be worth a second look down the road.

The pattern across all five: agree, ask one specific clarifying
question, then react based on the answer. **Never push back. Never
defend.** Disarmed prospects open up; defended-against prospects
close.

## §5. The "second-no" reactivation play

The closed-lost pile is your most valuable lead source. A prospect
who said "no" once already knows your offering, knows your name, and
in 80% of cases said "not now" rather than "not ever."

The reactivation cadence runs 90, 180, and 365 days after the polite
close (Touch 7):

### 90-day touch — "checking in"

```
Hey [name], it's been a quarter since we talked about [their topic].
No agenda — just curious how things have evolved on your end and
whether the situation that made it "not the right time" has changed.
If you want a no-pressure update on what we're working on, just hit
reply.
```

### 180-day touch — "new thing"

```
Hey [name], we shipped [a specific new capability / case study / pricing
change] since we last talked. Sharing in case it changes the math on
[their topic]. No reply needed if it doesn't.
```

### 365-day touch — "anniversary"

```
Hey [name], it's been a year since we first connected about [their
topic]. I'd love a 5-minute update on how you ended up solving it
(or whether it's still on your list). Either way, no agenda — just
genuinely curious.
```

After the 365-day touch, the prospect is either: (a) re-engaged and
back in the active cadence, or (b) permanently retired from outbound.
Either is a clean outcome.

## §6. Channel mix — why we use email + phone + LinkedIn

A single-channel cadence is a fragile cadence. Email goes to spam.
Phones get screened. LinkedIn DMs get missed. The triple-channel mix
guarantees that *some* version of your message reaches the prospect.

The split, per touch:

| Touch | Email | Phone | LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ✓ | | |
| 2 | ✓ | | |
| 3 | ✓ | | |
| 4 | | ✓✓ (voicemail × 2) | |
| 5 | | | ✓ |
| 6 | ✓ | | |
| 7 | ✓ | | |

Don't add channels for their own sake. If your prospects don't use
LinkedIn, skip Touch 5. If your prospects don't answer phones, the
voicemails become emails. The principle is the discipline, not the
specific channel mix.

## §7. Tools & templates

| Function | Lightweight option | Pro-grade option |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence tracking | Spreadsheet with date triggers | HubSpot Sequences / Outreach / Apollo |
| Email send | Gmail with canned responses | CRM mail merge / SDR tool |
| Phone | Your cell + voice notes app | Dialer (Aircall, Dialpad) with call logging |
| LinkedIn | Manual | Sales Navigator + automation tool (use sparingly — bans risk) |
| Reactivation pile | Sheet tab "Closed-Lost > 90d" | CRM "lost reason" + nurture pipeline |

The lightweight stack is enough to run this playbook for 50+ active
deals/month. Don't upgrade until specific friction proves you need
to.

## §8. Metrics & KPIs

Track three numbers, weekly:

1. **Median touches per deal.** Target: 5+.
   Below 3 = leaving money on the table.
2. **Cadence completion rate.** % of deals that reach Touch 7 (or
   close before). Target: 90%+. A low number means you're abandoning
   cadences mid-flight — the most common failure mode.
3. **"Second-no" reactivation rate.** % of closed-lost deals that
   re-engage in the 90/180/365 day touches. Target: 5-10%.

The single chart that matters: a stacked bar showing your active
pipeline by cadence stage (T1, T2, … T7). At any glance, you can see
which deals are due for which touch.

## §9. The 30/60/90-day rollout

### Days 1–30: Install the cadence

- Run the diagnostic (§2). Note your starting median-touches number.
- Build the 7-touch templates in your email tool. Save as canned
  responses or sequences.
- Build the active-cadence tracker (one row per deal, columns for
  T1-T7 dates, status, notes).
- Apply the new cadence to *every new quoted deal*. Don't retrofit
  old deals yet.

### Days 31–60: Add the voicemail + LinkedIn branches

- Record the two voicemail templates so they're ready to deliver
  consistently without having to think on the spot.
- Identify which prospects are reachable on LinkedIn. Skip Touch 5
  for those who aren't.
- Start the reactivation play on the *oldest* closed-lost deals
  (90+ days). Easy wins.

### Days 61–90: Tune

- Re-run the diagnostic on deals closed in the last 30 days. Compare
  the new median-touches to the baseline.
- Compute your "cadence completion rate." If it's below 80%, find out
  why — usually it's because the cadence got buried in your inbox,
  not because the deals are bad. Build a Friday reminder ritual.
- Review the reactivation pile. Any reactivation wins yet? Capture
  what worked so you can repeat.

## §10. Common failure modes

Three patterns that kill this playbook:

1. **The cadence dies in week 2.** You start strong, then by Touch 4
   you've got 15 deals at different stages of the cadence and you can't
   keep them straight. Fix: ruthlessly build a Friday review where you
   schedule every touch for the coming week. Make it a 30-min ritual,
   not an ad-hoc memory exercise.
2. **Every touch sounds the same.** If your Touch 2 and Touch 5 read
   like the same email with different paragraphs, the prospect tunes
   out. Each touch must have a *different* purpose: confirm, give,
   ask, voice, dimension, surface, close. Mix the channels too.
3. **You don't retire.** You keep adding Touch 8, 9, 10 because you
   "don't want to give up." This burns the relationship and the
   reactivation upside. Touch 7 is the hard stop; the 90/180/365 day
   touches are the patient long-game.

## §11. What's NOT in this playbook

- **Discovery call mechanics.** This playbook assumes a quote has
  already been sent. Pre-quote discovery is a different engagement.
- **Closing the sale on the call.** Live-objection-handling on a
  phone call is its own discipline — see the voice sales tactics doc
  for that.
- **Cold prospecting.** This is follow-up after a warm interaction.
  Cold outbound is a separate playbook.
